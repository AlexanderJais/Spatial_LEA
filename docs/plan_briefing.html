Findings · Xenium spatial transcriptomics · cohort A

Galanin receptor signalling in the ageing mediobasal hypothalamus

A 32-type MBH cell atlas with Galr1 measured 23–30× above background — and, at 2 aged vs 2 adult, no systematic age difference in the receptor. The ligand moves more than the receptor does.

4 male mice · 2 aged (15–16 mo) / 2 adult (7 mo) 40,783 QC-passing MBH cells 297-gene mBrain panel 17 Aug 2026
Headline

Galr1 does not change systematically with age in these animals

Across 16 cell types with enough expressing cells to support a fold change, the two independent batches agree on the direction of the Galr1 age effect in 8 of 16 (detection rate) and 10 of 16 (expression level). That is what two coin flips give — binomial p = 0.60 and 0.23. Nothing in Galr1 or Galr3 survives a shortlist of "consistent in both blocks and at least 1.5× in the weaker block".

This is a real result, not a failed measurement: Galr1 is detected in 15–19% of cells at 23–30× the strongest negative-control probe, and the atlas is clean. The age contrast simply is not there at this sample size.

What did hold up

Three findings worth carrying forward

Lead 1 · receptor

The GABA Gal/Galr1 population — 1,705 cells, 53–67% Galr1⁺, with Galr1 as a defining marker — is ~22–24% lower in aged, consistent in both blocks and both metrics. The one Galr1 lead.

Lead 2 · ligand

Tanycyte Gal falls ~2× in aged (LFC −0.87 / −0.89 detection; −0.67 / −1.35 expression). The tightest, most reproducible effect in the whole analysis.

Lead 3 · source shift

ARC Agrp/Npy Gal rises ~1.8× in aged. Together with the tanycyte drop this points at a change in galanin availability and source, not receptor abundance.

A shift in where galanin comes from, with receptor levels broadly held constant, is a more mechanistic story than a receptor count — and it is testable directly with the spatial data.

Caveat that changes what can be claimed

The DMH result is probably geometry, not biology

DMH Grp/Ppp1r17 neurons look like a dramatic ageing phenotype — 1.57% and 1.22% of ROI cells in the aged animals versus 0.46% and 0.10% in the adults, consistent in both blocks, up to a 16× range. It should not be reported as one yet.

If the aged sections were simply cut more caudally, DMH and Hcrt should both rise. DMH rises 16× while Hcrt falls — so this is not one clean cut-level shift but a mix of section plane and ROI placement. The hand-drawn rectangles differ in area by 40% (3.68–5.15 mm²), so how much DMH versus LHA each box captures is partly a drawing decision. Composition correlation is r = 0.934 within group versus 0.905 between — group differences barely exceed section-to-section noise.

The fix

Assign every cell to an anatomical nucleus, then express abundance as a fraction of cells within that nucleus rather than within the ROI. That removes box geometry from the denominator entirely, and it is the only way to answer "is the effect in the DMH?" properly.

Foundations

The atlas and the measurement are publication-grade

Data quality

99.7–100% of ROI cells pass QC. Negative-control probe rate 0.012% of counts, no multinucleate segmentations, 201–269 median counts per cell.

Atlas

32 cell types, 16/16 canonical markers enriched in their assigned class; median animal-mixing entropy 0.99. All expected MBH populations recovered.

Ageing signature

Gfap, Cd68, Trem2 up consistently in both blocks (+0.24 to +0.30 log2). Modest — as expected at 15–16 months.

Study design

A randomised complete block design

Age is orthogonal to run day, slide and cassette — each batch carries one aged and one adult animal. Every effect above is estimated as a within-block fold change, one per batch, and the evidence is whether the two agree.

Two things to state plainly in the manuscript

At 15.0 and 16.1 months the "aged" animals are middle-aged by the NIA convention, not old — put the ages in the abstract and scope the claim to mid-life onset.

With 2 vs 2 blocked, the exact permutation null has four label assignments, so the smallest attainable two-sided p-value is 0.5 for any gene at any effect size. Everything above is reported as effect size and cross-block consistency; no p-value is claimed for a single cell type.

Next, in order of value

What moves this toward a publishable claim

  1. Nucleus assignment

    Registration to the Allen CCF plus spatial-domain clustering and marker anchors, reconciled — with a continuous distance-to-3rd-ventricle frame alongside. Removes the ROI-geometry confound and answers the DMH question properly. Needs the DAPI images for proper registration.

  2. The other six sections

    Each animal has 2–3 sections; only one per animal has an ROI. Adding them stabilises every per-animal estimate and directly tests whether the DMH result survives a different cut. Either you draw the ROIs or we propagate them by landmark registration for your approval.

  3. Cohort B as independent replication

    Different pipeline version and segmentation, so it cannot be pooled — but if it is also adult vs aged, agreement across two independently processed cohorts is worth more than pooling would be. Needs its ages and groups.

  4. More animals, and ideally older ones

    An effect the size of the tanycyte Gal drop would need roughly n = 5–8 per group to be demonstrable. An 18–24-month group would turn the middle-aged caveat into an ageing trajectory.
